Does a better event-free survival mean more time free of treatment?

In perioperative immunotherapy trials, event-free survival (EFS) is measured from randomisation and is the primary basis for regulatory approval and clinical adoption. But EFS is blind to a structural asymmetry: a patient in the perioperative arm who remains in remission is receiving adjuvant therapy, while an equivalent patient in the neoadjuvant-only arm is not.

This tool makes that asymmetry visible. Enter the three medians available from any published trial — EFS, OS, and planned treatment duration — and the treatment-free interval (TFI) is derived for each arm. The question it answers: does the EFS advantage actually translate into more time free of systemic therapy, or does it merely convert treatment-free time into adjuvant therapy time?
